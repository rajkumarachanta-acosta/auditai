## 2026-08-02 — Topic: ACOS vs. contribution margin (metric blind spots)

**Angle tag:** `metric-blindspot`, `founder-questions`, `contrarian`

**Hook:** "Your ACOS is 18%. Your agency is thrilled. Your CFO shouldn't be."

**Post:**

Your ACOS is 18%. Your agency is thrilled. Your CFO shouldn't be.

Most Amazon sellers optimize the wrong number.

ACOS measures ad efficiency. It says nothing about whether the business is actually making money.

I've audited enough accounts to see the same gap every time: a campaign report showing 18% ACOS sitting on top of a P&L showing a 30%+ return rate, thin margins, and a handful of ASINs carrying 80% of revenue while ad budget gets spread evenly across all of them.

None of that shows up in the metric your agency reports on.

Three questions worth asking before your next QBR:

1. What % of ad spend touched a keyword that generated zero sales this month? Not "low ACOS" — zero.
2. What's blended TACOS — total ad spend over total revenue — not just attributed ACOS?
3. Which 20% of ASINs are absorbing 80% of the ad budget, and is that where the margin actually is?

A campaign can hit every ACOS target and still bleed cash if the wrong products are getting funded or returns are quietly eating the sale.

Ad efficiency is a tactic. Contribution margin is the outcome. Founders who only see the first number are flying with half the instrument panel.
