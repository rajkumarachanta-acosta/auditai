## 2026-08-02 — Topic: Q4 prep — inventory/ad-spend sync (timing risk)

**Angle tag:** `q4-prep`, `inventory-ad-sync`, `story`

**Hook:** "Every Q4 disaster I've audited started in October. The mistake was actually made in August."

**Post:**

Every Q4 disaster I've audited started in October. The mistake was actually made in August.

Two accounts, same category, same ad budget, same Prime Big Deal Days:

Brand A treated ads and inventory as separate workstreams. Ops ordered stock off a sales forecast. Marketing set budgets off a media plan. Nobody synced the two until the campaigns were already live and outbidding demand they couldn't fulfill.

Brand B did one thing differently in August: they mapped ad spend velocity to inventory coverage, ASIN by ASIN, before a single bid went up. When a hero SKU had 6 weeks of stock left and Q4 needed 10, they throttled that campaign in September — not December, when the buy box already showed "temporarily out of stock" and organic rank started sliding.

Brand A's CFO got a Q4 that looked like a win on the ad dashboard and a loss on the P&L: high spend, high ACOS on the SKUs that stocked out, and a rank hit that bled into January.

If your ad plan and your inventory plan live in separate spreadsheets right now, that's not an ops problem. It's a Q4 revenue problem, and August is the last month to fix it while it's still cheap.

## 2026-08-02 — Topic: ACOS vs. contribution margin (metric blind spots)

**Angle tag:** `metric-blindspot`, `founder-questions`, `contrarian`

**Hook:** "Your ACOS is 18%. Your agency is thrilled. Your CFO shouldn't be."

**Post:**

Your ACOS is 18%. Your agency is thrilled. Your CFO shouldn't be.

Most Amazon sellers optimize the wrong number.

ACOS measures ad efficiency. It says nothing about whether the business is actually making money.

I've audited enough accounts to see the same gap every time: a campaign report showing 18% ACOS sitting on top of a P&L showing a 30%+ return rate, thin margins, and a handful of ASINs carrying 80% of revenue while ad budget gets spread evenly across all of them.

None of that shows up in the metric your agency reports on.

Three questions worth asking before your next QBR:

1. What % of ad spend touched a keyword that generated zero sales this month? Not "low ACOS" — zero.
2. What's blended TACOS — total ad spend over total revenue — not just attributed ACOS?
3. Which 20% of ASINs are absorbing 80% of the ad budget, and is that where the margin actually is?

A campaign can hit every ACOS target and still bleed cash if the wrong products are getting funded or returns are quietly eating the sale.

Ad efficiency is a tactic. Contribution margin is the outcome. Founders who only see the first number are flying with half the instrument panel.
